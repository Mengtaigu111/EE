You are an expert in scientific event extraction.

TASK
Read the full abstract and identify the scientific events whose triggers occur
in the TARGET SENTENCE. Explain each event using the relevant argument text
and semantic roles, then identify its event type. Finally, report the number
of events of each type.

Use the full abstract for context. Arguments may appear anywhere in the full
abstract, but include only events whose triggers occur in the target sentence.

EVENT TYPE DEFINITIONS

PUR — Purpose
Expresses what a study or an operation aims to address, accomplish, or achieve.
Aim is the goal. Distinguish an intended purpose from an already established
result.

ITT — IntroduceTarget
Introduces or defines the object, task, or problem that the research focuses
on. The focus is on "what is being studied," as distinct from PUR's "what is
intended to be achieved."

RWS — RelatedWorkStep
Describes how existing methods, procedures, or components work, and the
relationships between the relevant objects and components.

RWF — RelatedWorkFault
Describes shortcomings, difficulties, limitations, or defects in the objects,
methods, or practices involved in the research. Concern is the object with
the problem, and Fault is the specific problem.

PRP — Propose
Expresses that the authors propose or introduce a contribution. The focus is
on "what is proposed." Do not classify an event solely on the basis of trigger
words such as "propose."

WKS — WorkStatement
States what research work the authors have carried out, including studying,
building, analyzing, and evaluating. The focus is on a statement of research
work. Content involving a method operation does not automatically make the
event MDS.

MDS — MethodStep
States how the method presented in the paper operates or is implemented,
including operational steps, the use of components, and functional
relationships between components. The focus is on method implementation
relationships, rather than merely reporting that the authors performed some
work.

FIN — Finding
Expresses what the authors observe, discover, prove, or conclude. The focus
is on the finding relationship. Its Content may also separately form a FAC
or CMP event.

CMP — ExperimentCompare
Expresses an explicit comparison between two objects in an experiment or
evaluation. Arg1 and Arg2 are the compared objects, Result is the comparison
result, and Metrics and Extent describe the metric and degree.

FAC — OutcomeFact
States a result-related fact, effect, or property that an object or method
produces, exhibits, or possesses, and may include relationships involving
Subject, Object, Target, Condition, Extent, Reason, and other roles.

These definitions describe the semantic focus of each event type. Do not
classify events solely on the basis of keywords, the subject, tense, or a
single argument. Not all listed roles are required to appear. A sentence may
contain multiple events, including multiple event instances of the same type.
