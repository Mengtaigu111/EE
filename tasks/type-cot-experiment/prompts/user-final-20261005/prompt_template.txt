EVENT TYPE DEFINITIONS

PUR — Purpose
Expresses what the research or an operation aims to address, accomplish, or
achieve. Aim represents the intended goal. Distinguish an intended purpose
from a result or fact that has already been established.

ITT — IntroduceTarget
Introduces or defines the object, task, or problem that the research focuses
on. The semantic focus is "what is being studied," rather than PUR's "what is
intended to be achieved."

RWS — RelatedWorkStep
Describes how existing methods, procedures, or components work, including
relationships among the relevant objects, methods, and components.

RWF — RelatedWorkFault
Describes a shortcoming, difficulty, limitation, or defect in an object,
method, or practice involved in the research. Concern is the object that has
the problem, while Fault is the specific problem.

PRP — Propose
Expresses that the authors propose or introduce a contribution. The semantic
focus is "what is proposed." Classification should be based on the semantic
relationship rather than words such as "propose" or "introduce" alone.

WKS — WorkStatement
States what research work the authors carry out, such as studying,
constructing, analyzing, or evaluating something. The semantic focus is a
statement of research work. Content involving a method operation does not
automatically make the event MDS.

MDS — MethodStep
Describes how the method presented in the paper operates or is implemented,
including operational steps, use of components, and functional relationships
between components. The semantic focus is the method's implementation or
internal operation, rather than merely reporting work performed by the
authors.

FIN — Finding
Expresses what the authors observe, discover, demonstrate, prove, or conclude.
The semantic focus is the finding relationship. The Content of a FIN event may
also separately correspond to a FAC or CMP event.

CMP — ExperimentCompare
Expresses an explicit comparison between two objects in an experiment or
evaluation. Arg1 and Arg2 are the compared objects. Result represents the
comparison result, while Metrics and Extent describe the evaluation metric
and degree.

FAC — OutcomeFact
States a result-related fact, effect, or property that an object or method
produces, exhibits, or possesses. The event may involve roles such as Subject,
Object, Target, Condition, Extent, and Reason.

These definitions describe the semantic focus of each event type. Do not
classify events solely on the basis of keywords, the subject, tense, or a
single argument. Not all listed roles are required to appear. A sentence may
contain multiple events, including multiple event instances of the same type.
